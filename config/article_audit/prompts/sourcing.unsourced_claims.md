Check: unsourced claims (WP:V, WP:CITE, WP:MINREF).

Find statements in the article that need an inline citation and don't have one. A statement is covered when a citation (a <ref> tag or a citation template) follows it in the same sentence, or follows a later sentence in the same paragraph that the statement plainly depends on.

Statements that need a citation include:
- direct quotations
- statistics, dates, figures and other specific facts a reader could challenge
- claims about living people, especially anything contentious (WP:BLP), which is must_fix
- opinions, evaluations, and claims of significance, priority or influence ("the first", "widely regarded", "a major influence")
- medical or health claims (WP:MEDRS)
- any claim likely to be challenged

Statements that generally don't need their own citation:
- the lead, when it summarizes body content that is cited (but flag lead claims that appear nowhere in the body)
- plot summaries of the work the article is about
- common knowledge that no reasonable reader would challenge

Report each unsourced statement or group of related unsourced statements as a finding, quoting the statement. Report a whole section or paragraph that has no citations at all as one finding. Don't comment on whether the existing citations are reliable or correctly formatted; other checks cover that.
