Check: section structure and weight (MOS:LAYOUT, WP:DUE, WP:SUMMARY, WP:NOT).

Judge whether the article's sections suit its subject and give each aspect appropriate weight. Start by deciding what type of article this is (for example a biography, a disease or medical topic, a species, an organization, a place, a book or other creative work, an event, or a general concept) and apply the conventions for that type, such as WP:MEDMOS section order for medical articles. Look for:
- sections that articles of this type normally have and that are missing, when the omission is significant
- headings that don't fit this type of article, or sections in an unconventional order
- undue weight: sections that are disproportionately long or detailed for a minor aspect, or brief for a major one
- biographies that read like a CV (WP:NOTCV), especially academics: long lists of publications, awards or positions instead of prose about the person's significant work
- lists or bullet points where prose would be more appropriate (MOS:PROSE)
- content duplicated in two places in the article, excluding the lead's summary
- content that belongs in a broader parent article, or detail that should be summarized because a separate article covers it (WP:SUMMARY)
- travel-guide or directory material such as opening hours, prices, fees or directions (WP:NOTGUIDE)
- licensing, certification, degree or other requirements given for one country without saying which country they apply to

For a sandbox draft of a section to be added to an existing article, judge the draft as that section. Leave out problems with the lead, sourcing, tone and style; other checks cover those.
