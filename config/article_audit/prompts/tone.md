Check: tone and neutrality (WP:NPOV, WP:PEACOCK, WP:WEASEL, WP:EDITORIALIZING, MOS:WTW).

Find language that doesn't meet Wikipedia's standard of a neutral, encyclopedic, fact-based tone. Look for:
- Promotional or peacock language: puffery that praises rather than informs ("renowned", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "groundbreaking", "boasts").
- Weasel words and unsupported attributions: vague sources standing in for named ones ("some say", "experts believe", "it is widely thought", "critics have argued" with no critics named).
- Editorializing: words that impose a view or steer the reader ("notably", "clearly", "unfortunately", "it should be noted", "interestingly", "of course").
- Analysis or argument in Wikipedia's voice: the article draws its own conclusions, synthesizes an argument, or evaluates the subject, rather than reporting what reliable sources say and attributing opinions to the people who hold them.
- Essay or academic-paper style: thesis statements, first or second person, rhetorical questions, "this article will discuss", conclusions sections.
- Balance: significant viewpoints from reliable sources that are missing or given too little weight, one side presented as fact, or criticism and praise out of proportion to the sources (WP:DUE). Report balance problems only when the gap is apparent from the article itself, and say what kind of perspective seems to be missing rather than asserting specific facts.

Quote the specific wording that is the problem. Where a neutral rewording is possible without adding facts, give it as the suggestion. Don't report problems with sourcing, structure or style here; other checks cover those.
