Check: the lead section (MOS:LEAD, MOS:BOLDLEAD, MOS:LEADLENGTH).

The lead is the text before the first section heading. Check that it:
- opens by defining the subject in plain terms, with the article title or subject name in bold in the first sentence where that reads naturally (MOS:BOLDLEAD)
- summarizes the most important points of the whole article, including any prominent controversies, rather than introducing the topic like an essay
- establishes why the subject is notable
- contains nothing that isn't covered in more detail in the body; quote each lead statement that has no counterpart in the body
- is in proportion to the article: about one paragraph for a short article, up to about four for a long one
- avoids citations-heavy detail, lists, and material that belongs in the body

For a sandbox draft that is a section to be added to an existing article, rather than a whole article, report only problems with any lead text the draft adds, and don't fault it for lacking a lead. Leave out problems with headings, sourcing, tone and the body's structure; other checks cover those.
